# Masar (مسار) — KSA / GCC Executive-Education Market & Brand Research

Date: 2026-09-06. Prepared as a brand-strategy brief for a premium executive-education marketplace launching KSA-first, then global.

Method note: the session proxy blocked every `.sa` domain (misk.org.sa, mbsc.edu.sa, ipa.edu.sa, alfaisal.edu, kaust.edu.sa, hrdf.org.sa, dga.gov.sa) plus most agency/portfolio hosts, so site-look observations below come from (a) search-engine snippets of those sites, (b) third-party descriptions (Saudipedia, agency case studies, brand-guideline portals), (c) GitHub-hosted font metadata for verification of typeface facts, and (d) prior domain knowledge — flagged "(not directly verified this session)" where relevant. Treat hex codes attributed to third-party brands as approximate unless a source is cited.

### 1.11 How the trusted Saudi sites *look* (pattern summary)
- Bilingual with an **AR|EN toggle in the header**, Arabic default for government (IPA, HRDF, NeLC), English default for MBSC/KAUST/Alfaisal exec-ed (their buyers are English-comfortable and the faculty is international).
- Government/quasi-government sites follow the **DGA Platforms Code** look: white ground, one restrained brand colour, IBM Plex Sans Arabic, big card grids, prominent e-service CTAs, footer with licence/accessibility statements.
- University exec-ed sites are conservative: hero photograph of a campus or a seated cohort, PDF brochure download, programme cards (title / dates / duration / language / fee / "Register interest"), partner-school logos, dean's message, "Corporate Solutions" section, contact by phone/email/form.
- What they do *not* do: no hard-sell pricing anchors, no countdown timers, no fake urgency, very few testimonials; authority comes from patronage, partners and eligibility criteria.

## 2. Saudi digital design conventions that signal credibility

### 2.1 DGA "Platforms Code" (the national design system, Nov 2024)
- Announced by the Digital Government Authority as "the national reference for designing and developing government platform interfaces"; open-source; Figma components library published to the community; React/Angular/OutSystems ports exist; official site design.dga.gov.sa.
- Typography: **IBM Plex Sans Arabic** for UI, paired with **IBM Plex Sans** (Latin) and the Ministry of Culture's **"Saudi" typeface** for display/identity (third-party implementer reports).
- Principles: full RTL, bilingual typography, WCAG-grade accessibility, consistent terminology, standardized date/number formats.
  - https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2209319 · https://www.figma.com/community/file/1392264328585493958/components-library-platforms-code · https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q127850597 · https://dga-registry.vercel.app/ · https://www.outsystems.com/forge/component-overview/20754/dga-ui-o11 · https://oss.dga.gov.sa/en/products/dga-ac319-national-design-system
- Implication: Saudi executives now see Plex Sans Arabic on Absher, Tawakkalna-era services, ministries — it reads "official and safe". Using it for body copy borrows that trust; using it *alone* risks looking like a government portal.

### 2.2 Ministry of Culture typefaces (free for organisations, not on Google Fonts)
- 2023 "Font Initiative": **Masmak** (sturdy, legible; named after Masmak fort), **Al-Naseeb** (handwritten), **Watad** (tent-peg, festival use). 2025: **Al-Awwal** (first-Hijri-century inscriptions) and **Saudi** ("built on the foundations of traditional calligraphy… suitable for logos, headlines, official documents"). All free for individuals/organisations; download via engage.moc.gov.sa/fonts and moc.gov.sa.
  - https://www.arabnews.com/node/2272171/saudi-arabia · https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2300137 · https://www.moc.gov.sa/en/Modules/Pages/ArabicFonts/Saudi · https://destinationksa.com/en/new-arabic-typefaces/
- Implication: "Saudi" or "Masmak" for Arabic display is the single most Saudi-rooted typographic move available at zero licence cost; plan it as the phase-2 upgrade from the Google-Fonts pairing below (self-host, confirm terms of use).

### 2.5 RTL / bilingual layout norms (what Saudi users check in the first 5 seconds)
- Rebuild the grid for RTL; don't mirror blindly: navigation starts at the right, primary CTA sits at the *start* (right) of a row, progress flows right→left, carets/chevrons and "back" arrows flip, but **logos, phone numbers, brand icons, media controls, clocks and charts' axes do not flip**.
- Numerals: use Western Arabic digits (0-9) in KSA UI (Saudi official platforms do); Eastern digits (٠-٩) only if the user opts in. Keep numbers, SAR amounts, emails and URLs LTR inside RTL text (`unicode-bidi: isolate`/`dir="ltr"` spans).
- Arabic type needs ~10–15% larger size and looser line-height than the Latin at the same visual weight; never fake-bold; never letter-space Arabic; never all-caps equivalents (there are none, so don't use tracking-based "caps" styling in Arabic headings).
- Language switcher in a consistent header position labelled in the *target* language ("English" / "العربية"), never flags. Arabic version must have **equal depth** of content; users notice thinner Arabic pages and rank the brand down.
- Forms: right-aligned labels and inputs with `dir="rtl"`, but phone/ID/IBAN fields LTR. Mobile touch targets ≥44 px; >90% of social and most web sessions are mobile (99% internet penetration; 95%+ smartphone).
- Sources: https://cloudtopia.net/articles/best-website-design-practices-for-rtl-arabic-layouts-in-2026 · https://bycomsolutions.com/blog/arabic-rtl-web-design-best-practices/ · https://hamrix.com/ksa/blog/arabic-rtl-ui-ux-design-guide · https://makitsol.com/arabic-ux-design-fixes-for-gcc-rtl-websites/ · https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-saudi-arabia

- Modern Standard Arabic, formal register, no dialect in marketing; honorifics matter in outreach ("سعادة", "معالي", "الدكتور/المهندس"); err toward formality; business cards Arabic on one side.
  - https://www.babelgroup.co.uk/resources/doing-business-in/doing-business-in-saudi-arabia/ · https://abriggs.com/blog/business-etiquette-saudi-arabia · https://www.bridge-connect.com/post/business-etiquette-saudi-arabia-practical-guide-visitors-and-investors

## 3. Buyer behaviour

### 3.1 Demand drivers (Vision 2030 priority sectors → leadership skills)
- Sectors: tourism & entertainment (Riyadh Season, Diriyah, AlUla, Red Sea), sports/mega-events (FIFA 2034, Expo 2030), renewable energy & mining, logistics, healthcare, financial services (PIF portfolio, Tadawul listings), technology/AI (SDAIA), manufacturing, giga-projects (NEOM, Qiddiya). CSIS (mid-2026): Vision 2030 ambitions "have outpaced Saudi Arabia's ability to produce Saudi workers with the skill sets necessary". Skills named: critical thinking, cross-cultural collaboration, agile project/change leadership; localisation targets mean these "must be developed internally, at scale, and fast".
  - https://www.csis.org/analysis/saudi-vision-2030-ten · https://disprz.ai/blog/workforce-transformation-saudi-2030 · https://www.optivn.com/en/blogs/leadership-development-saudi-arabia
- Market size: corporate training USD 3.59B (2024) → USD 6.66B by 2033 (IMARC); executive education & leadership training USD 2.3B (2025) → 4.14B (2031), ~1.08M paid participant engagements, **Riyadh = ~64% of revenue** (Ken Research); Middle East exec-ed USD 9.4B by 2030 (IndustryARC). Government committed USD 20B to data/AI skilling by 2030.
  - https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-corporate-training-market · https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/saudi-arabia-executive-education-and-leadership-training-market · https://contentenginellc.com/2024/03/14/middle-east-executive-education-and-corporate-training-market-size-worth-94-billion-by-2030-industryarc/
- Practical skill themes selling in 2026: AI for executives, board governance (new SOE/PIF-company boards), transformation/PMO leadership, ESG/sustainability, family-business succession, tourism/hospitality leadership, public-sector leadership (KPI/performance culture), women's leadership.

### 3.2 Who buys and how
- **Individuals** (Saudi professionals, 25–44 heavy, 10.9M LinkedIn users = 28% of population): want a recognised certificate name, HRDF reimbursement eligibility, Arabic option, flexible instalments (Tamara/Tabby BNPL lifts conversion 10–25% for SAR 200–1,500 baskets; Sharia-compliant, up to 24 months on Tamara), Apple Pay/mada.
  - https://stats.napoleoncat.com/linkedin-users-in-saudi_arabia/2025/07/ · https://logiolegion.com/blogs/bnpl-integration-ecommerce-checkout-saudi-arabia-tamara-tabby · https://tamara.co/en-sa
- **Corporate HR/L&D**: buys cohorts and custom programmes; expects a PDF portfolio, a "corporate solutions" contact, invoices with CR + VAT number (ZATCA e-invoicing/Fatoora), proposals in Arabic for approvals, Saudization/Nitaqat alignment, and ability to report training hours to Qiwa/HRDF.
- **Government entities**: procure through Etimad (supplier must hold CR; registration free), often via IPA-equivalent or "training plan" approvals; want closed cohorts, Arabic delivery, IPA-recognised or ETEC-accredited certificates, and attendance/assessment reports for civil-service HR files.
  - https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/saudi-arabia-selling-public-sector · https://nextbidsa.com/en/blog/what-is-etimad-saudi-government-procurement-guide

### 3.3 Certificates and accreditation
- Saudi employers "prefer… accredited professional certificates"; TVTC accreditation is read as "a guarantee that the institute follows the highest quality standards… certificates recognised by employers in public and private sectors". HRDF reimburses only certifications on its list. Foreign degrees need MoE equivalency; short-course certificates don't, but **the issuing school's name and seal** carry the value.
  - https://curetraining.com/professional-certificates-in-saudi-arabia/ · https://mdit.edu.sa/en/blog/institutes-accredited-by-the-general-organization-for-technical-and-vocational-training · https://www.moe.gov.sa/en/knowledgecenter/eservices/pages/certeq.aspx
- Implication: every listing needs a "Certificate" block: issuer, seal preview, verification URL/QR, hours (CPD/PDU), accreditation/licence numbers, HRDF-list status.

### 3.4 Contact preferences

## 4. Concrete direction for Masar (مسار)

### 4.1 Brand idea
"Masar" = path/track/course. The idea to own: **the considered route** — a curated, accredited path for leaders responsible for the Kingdom's transformation, not a catalogue. Visual metaphor: the line of a path across Najd — a single confident stroke (also echoes the elongated kashida in Arabic calligraphy and the horizontal "slider" stcForward made famous). Avoid: palm, falcon, date, crescent, Arabesque tile, gold foil, saudi-green.

### 4.2 Three candidate palettes (all avoid flat green+gold; each tested to keep body text ≥ 7:1 on its ground)

### 4.3 Typeface pairing (Google Fonts, verified)
- **Headlines, Arabic: Markazi Text** (variable 400–700; refined Naskh, editorial; matched Latin available for mixed headlines). Fallback: Noto Naskh Arabic.
- **Headlines, Latin: Fraunces** at opsz ≥ 48, wght 500–600, SOFT 0, WONK 0 (crisp, premium serif; not Playfair). Fallback: Georgia.
- **Body & UI, both scripts: IBM Plex Sans Arabic + IBM Plex Sans** (100–700; the DGA national-design-system face, so it reads official and is superbly hinted; italics unavailable in Arabic — never fake them).
- Rationale: Naskh headline + serif Latin gives the "school, not startup" register that HBS/INSEAD/LBS carry, while Plex body copy borrows the state's UI trust. This exact combination is *not* what government portals use (they are all-Plex), so it stays distinctive.
- Alternative modern pairing if the brand skews younger/fintech: **Zain** (200–900, Arabic+Latin, 2024) for headlines + Plex Sans Arabic body.
- Phase-2 upgrade: self-host the Ministry of Culture **"Saudi"** typeface for Arabic display (free for organisations; confirm terms) — the most Saudi-rooted type move available, and the same face the DGA system pairs with Plex.
- Type rules: Arabic headline size ×1.1 of Latin; line-height 1.6 Arabic body / 1.5 Latin; no tracking on Arabic; numerals Western in KSA; minimum 16 px Arabic body on mobile.

### 4.4 Tone of voice
English — *assured, specific, unhurried.* Short declaratives; verbs of consequence ("lead", "decide", "govern"); numbers over adjectives; never "unlock", "empower", "journey", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "seamless". Say who teaches, where, for how long, and what you leave with.
- Do: "Twelve seats. Riyadh, 14–16 October. Taught by INSEAD faculty. Certificate issued by INSEAD, listed by HRDF."
- Don't: "Unlock your leadership potential with our world-class programmes!"

Arabic — الفصحى المعاصرة، رصينة ودافئة، بلا لهجة، بلا مبالغة، بلا علامات تعجّب. مخاطبة بصيغة الجمع المحترمة عند الحاجة، واستخدام مفردات التحوّل باعتدال. الأفعال قبل الصفات، والأرقام قبل الوعود.
- مثال: "برنامج تنفيذي في الحوكمة لأعضاء مجالس الإدارات. الرياض، ١٤–١٦ أكتوبر ٢٠٢٦م. يقدّمه أعضاء هيئة تدريس من إنسياد. الشهادة صادرة من إنسياد ومدرجة ضمن قائمة صندوق تنمية الموارد البشرية (هدف)."
- تجنّب: "انطلق نحو القمة مع أفضل البرامج العالمية!"، الترجمة الحرفية للعبارات الإنجليزية ("رحلتك القيادية")، كلمة "دورة" للبرامج التنفيذية (استخدم "برنامج تنفيذي")، والتعريب الركيك لأسماء الجامعات (اعتمد الاسم العربي المستقر: هارفارد، وارتن، إنسياد، كلية لندن للأعمال، آي إم دي).
- Micro-copy pairs: "احجز مقعدًا / Reserve a seat" · "اطلب عرضًا للمنشأة / Request a corporate proposal" · "تحدّث معنا عبر واتساب / Message us on WhatsApp" · "تحقّق من الشهادة / Verify a certificate".

### 4.5 Must-have trust and conversion elements for KSA buyers
1. Footer legal block: CR number, VAT number, Maroof badge, physical Riyadh address, +966 landline, NeLC/TVTC/ETEC licence numbers where held, privacy policy under PDPL.
2. Certificate block on every listing: issuer + seal preview, verification QR/URL, CPD/PDU hours, HRDF-list eligibility badge ("مدعوم من هدف"), IPA/ETEC recognition where applicable.
3. Partner-school strip with real logos and *what each does* (delivers / accredits / co-designs) — no "as seen in" filler.
4. Programme facts table: dates (Hijri + Gregorian), city/venue or online, language of delivery, seats, faculty names with affiliations, fee in SAR incl. VAT, cancellation policy, Ramadan/Eid schedule notes.
5. WhatsApp Business (verified) + "Request a call" + corporate proposal form; response-time promise in Saudi office hours; Arabic-speaking advisor named with photo.
6. Payment: mada, Apple Pay, Visa/MC, Tamara/Tabby instalments for individuals; bank transfer + ZATCA-compliant e-invoice + PO upload for organisations; "pay after approval" option for government (Etimad-registered supplier statement).
7. Corporate/Government track page: closed cohorts, custom design, Arabic delivery, on-site at client, competency-framework mapping, attendance/assessment reporting, downloadable PDF portfolio.
8. Women's cohorts and female faculty filter; imagery of Saudi executives (men in thobe/shemagh, women in and out of abaya) shot in Riyadh/Jeddah/KAEC interiors, not stock deserts.
9. Real social proof in the Saudi idiom: client entity logos (with permission), alumni titles and employers ("مدير عام، هيئة…"), short quotes with full names and titles — or none at all.
10. Calendar-aware UX: cohort calendar avoids Ramadan/Eid/Hajj/summer; shows "بعد صلاة العشاء" evening slots during Ramadan; dual-date pickers.
11. Nafath login (phase 2) and a plain email/phone OTP now; Arabic-first URL structure (/ar default for KSA, /en), hreflang, Arabic SEO titles.
12. Speed and mobile: sub-2s on 4G, sticky bottom CTA bar on mobile (WhatsApp / Reserve), PDF brochure that opens in WhatsApp preview.

### 4.6 Five things that make a Saudi executive dismiss the site as "generic / AI-generated"
1. **Translated Arabic.** Levantine/Egyptian marketing phrasing, literal calques ("رحلتك القيادية", "افتح إمكاناتك"), dialect, wrong hamza/ta-marbuta, exclamation marks, mismatched Arabic depth versus English — all read as machine output.
2. **Wrong people, wrong place.** Stock photos of non-Saudi Arabs (Emirati kandura, Gulf-generic "sheikh" imagery), Western boardrooms, desert dunes and camels, or AI-rendered faces with six fingers and a ghutra on backwards. Saudis identify attire and interiors instantly.
3. **The template look.** Saudi-green + gold, Arabesque pattern overlays, purple SaaS gradients, three-column feature cards with emoji icons, countdown timers, "Trusted by 10,000+ leaders" with no names — the Tailwind-template fingerprint.
4. **No legal body.** Missing CR/VAT/Maroof, no Riyadh address or +966 number, USD pricing, no VAT line, no named faculty or issuing institution — looks like a drop-shipped course aggregator.
5. **Broken RTL and calendars.** Mirrored icons, LTR numbers breaking Arabic sentences, phone fields that flip, Gregorian-only dates, cohorts scheduled inside Ramadan/Eid, English-only certificate previews — proof nobody Saudi touched the product.

### 4.7 KSA-first, then global — sequencing notes
- Phase 1 (KSA): Arabic default, SAR, HRDF/ETEC/NeLC language, Riyadh + Jeddah + Khobar cohorts, WhatsApp desk, Etimad supplier status, corporate PDF portfolio, Saudi faculty and partner schools already present in KSA (INSEAD, LBS, Wharton, HEC, IMD, Esade, Georgetown).
- Phase 2 (GCC): AED/QAR/KWD pricing toggles, UAE-style English-default option, Dubai/Abu Dhabi/Doha venues, Arabic still first-class.
- Phase 3 (global): English default outside GCC, USD/EUR, keep the Saudi-rooted identity (palette A/type pairing travel well because they are editorial rather than folkloric) and lead with "designed in Riyadh" as a differentiator rather than hiding it.
